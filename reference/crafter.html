Spell Circle Grammar

How a circle of runes and inscriptions becomes one well-defined spell, when two circles count as the same spell, and what each new rune or mechanic adds to the space. Everything here is a working proposal for the prototype. Updated Nov 4 with the fixes from the BG3 coverage audit: three new rune pairs (named by Jared on Nov 5: amplification and diminishing, haste and slow, invisibility and revelation), a ground column, and new inscriptions and rules.

The three parts of a circle

A circle reads like a sentence. The center is the noun (the thing the spell makes), the ring holds the verbs (instant actions), and the inscriptions say where and when.

Where and when
Inscriptions
  • Shape, exactly one. No shape means a single target. The shape sets how many points the spell has. One shape aims at a spell (an enemy's shown next move or an ally's prepared spell) instead of a place.
  • Points: up to 3 extra points (a portal exit, a second summon, three chosen targets). They stack, one slot each, and the budget spreads across them.
  • Anchor (Nov 4): the area follows the unit at its point. A burst on you that follows you is an aura.
  • Steerable (Nov 4, needs lingering): move the effect each round for a bonus action.
  • Reach: more range.
  • Timing: delay, lingering. Lingering always means what the spell made lasts longer: ground on a place, a status on a unit, the thing itself, or the change to an enemy's next shown moves.
  • Filter: enemies only or allies only. Friendly fire is on otherwise. Not allowed on ground (decided Nov 4).
  • Weakest first (Nov 4): touches the units with the least health first, until the spell's strength runs out.
  • Trigger: one event the spell waits for. On an event with two units (an attack, a hit), harmful runes land on the enemy and beneficial ones on your side.
  • Tether (decided Nov 4: an inscription, not a rune): joins the first point to the next point, or to you. Four kinds, the same tether underneath, balanced separately: copy, redirect, transfer and leash.
  • Whole battlefield: a 3-slot shape for global modifications.

Inscriptions are a set, so their order never matters. They are learned, permanent and never traded. These are major inscriptions (for the whole circle). Minor inscriptions, attached to one ring rune, are proposed in the spell model doc and not in this builder yet.

What it makes
Center rune
  • Hit: the rune's plain effect at each point (every rune except portal, summon and protect). On an area with lingering, or a wall, it also leaves ground.
  • Gate (portal): a door between two points that lasts the round.
  • Body (summon): a summon at each point, or one on every hex of an area shape, with the power budget split between them.
  • Shield (protect): a shield, and whatever hits it.
  • Ground (Nov 4): any hit on an area with lingering, or a wall. The center sets its base trait and ring runes add more.

Each thing has hooks, the places where ring runes attach. A two-point thing with only one point uses you as the second. With three or more points, each point joins the next. A ring rune that matches the center adds strength to the center instead of acting on it. Where a rune has a choice of recipient, beneficial effects go to allies first and harmful ones to enemies first.

What it does
Ring runes
  • Plain effects fire, frost, mend, wound, and since Nov 4 haste, slow, invisibility and revelation. Their health and status changes add up.
  • Amplifiers protect, expose (damage and healing taken), and since Nov 4 amplification and diminishing (damage and healing given).
  • Movers push, pull, portal.
  • Makers summon, stone.
  • Removers dispel.

Whether order matters depends on the hook. On a summon, on ground and in a global modification, every ring rune becomes a trait, so order never matters. On a spell, movers and dispel keep their place and the rest are traits. On a shield, ring runes are traits that fire when it's hit: beneficial ones help the bearer, harmful ones hit the attacker. At a point, on a unit or through a gate, runes run as steps, and two neighbours swap freely only when neither changes something the other uses. The ring splits into one section per point and is read clockwise from the top.

      When two circles are the same spell

      The forge rewrites every circle into one canonical form before giving it an id. These steps run in order.

      1. Fill in defaults. No shape inscription means a single target.
      2. Sort the inscriptions. They are a set, so their order never matters.
      3. Merge duplicates. A rune that appears twice in a section becomes one rune with more strength, at its first position.
      4. Sort runes that don't depend on each other. Every hook entry declares what it reads and what it changes: positions, damage modifiers, health, statuses, terrain, or traits of a thing. Two neighbouring runes swap freely when neither changes something the other reads; changes to health, statuses, terrain, damage modifiers and traits simply add up. The forge puts swappable runes in alphabetical order. Opposite pairs that end up together cancel when their entries in that column are true opposites (fire and frost, mend and wound, protect and expose, and since Nov 4 amplification and diminishing, haste and slow, invisibility and revelation, at a point, on a unit, through a gate or on ground), and the forge flags them as wasted. In other columns the two entries are different traits and both apply.
      5. Keep everything else in place. Wherever runes run as steps, a remover keeps its place, and sections keep their point order.
      6. Hash what's left. That is the recipe id.

      To revisit while building: the read and change declarations. They live on each hook entry, not on the rune, because the same rune can be a step in one place and a trait in another. As a safety net, the balance test boards can run both orders of every neighbouring pair and flag any pair whose declarations say "swappable" but whose results differ, or the reverse. Changing a declaration changes recipe ids, so settle them before launch.

      Center table: what every rune does as the center

      The center rune decides what the spell makes: an effect, a gate, a summon or a shield. An effect that lingers on an area, or is cast as a wall, becomes ground. The last three columns say what the center does depending on where the spell is aimed: at the board (a hex, an area or a unit), across the whole battlefield, or at a spell. The six runes added Nov 4 are amplification, diminishing, haste, slow, invisibility and revelation (Jared named them Nov 5). The circle above's center is highlighted.

      Hook table: every ring rune on every kind of recipient

      Each column is a kind of center output that a ring rune modifies: an effect on a place, an effect on a unit, ground (added Nov 4), something passing through a gate, a summon, an altered spell, or a global effect (global modification). The "along a tether" column is different: it says what each rune sends along a tether inscription (copy, redirect or transfer decide how it travels). A summon, portal or protect center always uses its own column, wherever it is aimed; a plain-effect center uses the place, unit or global column; anything aimed at a spell uses the spell column.       How the space branches

      Distinct rings for one center and one shape, counted by building every ring and keeping one per recipe id. The same runes branch very differently depending on the hook they land on.

      Order matters most at a point, where movers, makers and amplifiers make positions count. On a summon, every ring is a set of traits, so the count is just the number of rune mixes. A new mover grows the space faster than a new plain effect. Multiply by the 18 center runes, 14 shapes and the inscription choices for the full space; the circle size set by Level caps how many of these a player can reach.
